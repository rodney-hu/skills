# Email Writing Templates

## The 1/3/1 Rhythm

Every prose block follows this pattern:
- **1** opener
- **3** explanation sentences
- **1** conclusion

This creates natural flow and keeps readers engaged.

## The 6 Proven Openers

Use these to start any 1/3/1 block:

### 1. Strong Declarative Statement
State something bold and true.

Example: "Most college students raise way too much money in their first round."

### 2. Thought-Provoking Question
Ask something that makes them think.

Example: "What if the reason investors keep ghosting you has nothing to do with your idea?"

### 3. Controversial Opinion
Challenge conventional wisdom.

Example: "Pitch decks are overrated—and they're probably killing your fundraise."

### 4. Moment In Time
Tell a quick story.

Example: "I watched a college founder pitch for $2M. The VC stopped him after slide 3."

### 5. Vulnerable Statement
Share something honest.

Example: "I used to think VCs wanted bold ambition. Turns out, they want proof you won't waste their money."

### 6. Weird, Unique Insight (MOST COMMON)
Share a surprising fact or observation.

Example: "Did you know that most angel investors would rather fund a $100K ask they believe in than a $1M ask they don't?"

## The 5-Section Email Structure

Every email (Days 1-5) has exactly 5 sections:

### Section 1: The Reminder
**Purpose:** Intro and preview today's content

**Template:**
```
Hey there!

Welcome to Day {X} of {Course Name}.

Today we're going to talk about Mistake #{X}: {Mistake}.

Specifically:
- The reason {audience} make this mistake
- Why this mistake leads to {negative outcomes}
- And how to fix it

Let's dive in.
```

### Section 2: The Mistake
**Purpose:** Explain what the mistake is and why it's bad

**Structure:**
- Header: `### The Mistake: {Mistake}`
- 1/3/1 prose block
- Transition sentence
- 3-5 outcome bullets

**Template:**
```
### The Mistake: {Mistake}

{Opener - use one of the 6 proven openers}

{Sentence 1 - expand on the opener}
{Sentence 2 - add more context}
{Sentence 3 - connect to the audience's experience}

{Conclusion - transition to bullets}

This mistake leads to the following negative outcomes:

- **{Outcome 1 Title}:** {1-3 sentences explaining this outcome with specifics}

- **{Outcome 2 Title}:** {1-3 sentences explaining this outcome with specifics}

- **{Outcome 3 Title}:** {1-3 sentences explaining this outcome with specifics}
```

### Section 3: The Reason This Happens
**Purpose:** Explain why people make this mistake

**Structure:**
- Header: `### The Reason This Happens:`
- 1/3/1 prose block
- Transition sentence
- 3-5 reason bullets

**Template:**
```
### The Reason This Happens:

{Opener}

{Sentence 1 - why this is common}
{Sentence 2 - what causes it}
{Sentence 3 - the underlying belief or situation}

{Conclusion - transition to bullets}

Here are some even more specific reasons why this mistake happens:

- **{Reason 1 Title}:** {1-3 sentences explaining this reason}

- **{Reason 2 Title}:** {1-3 sentences explaining this reason}

- **{Reason 3 Title}:** {1-3 sentences explaining this reason}
```

### Section 4: How To Fix It
**Purpose:** Give actionable steps to avoid the mistake

**Structure:**
- Header: `### How To Fix It`
- 1/3/1 prose block
- Transition sentence
- 3-5 action bullets

**Template:**
```
### How To Fix It

{Opener}

{Sentence 1 - the core solution}
{Sentence 2 - why this works}
{Sentence 3 - what changes when you do this}

{Conclusion - transition to bullets}

Instead of {mistake}, here's what you should do instead:

- **{Action 1 Title}:** {1-3 sentences with specific steps}

- **{Action 2 Title}:** {1-3 sentences with specific steps}

- **{Action 3 Title}:** {1-3 sentences with specific steps}
```

### Section 5: Check Your Inbox Tomorrow
**Purpose:** Recap and preview next day

**Template for Days 1-4:**
```
### Don't Forget To Check Your Inbox Tomorrow!

Phew, we covered a lot today.

I hope this gives you some clarity around why this mistake happens—and some action steps to avoid it in the future.

And stay tuned for tomorrow, because we're going to dig into Mistake #{X+1}: {Next Mistake}.

Specifically:
- The reason {audience} make this mistake
- Why this mistake leads to {negative outcomes}
- And how to fix it

Keep an eye on your inbox!

Best,
{Your Name}
```

**Template for Day 5:**
```
### This Is The Last Email!

We've covered a lot over the past 5 days.

I hope you now have a clear picture of the 5 biggest mistakes {audience} make—and more importantly, how to avoid them.

Now go apply what you learned.

Best,
{Your Name}
```

## Bullet Guidelines

**Each bullet should be 1-3 sentences long.**

Not single lines. Not paragraphs. 1-3 sentences.

❌ Too short: "You need to validate first"
✅ Good: "You need to validate first. Talk to 20 potential customers before writing any code. If they won't commit to paying when you launch, keep iterating the idea."

❌ Too long: {8 sentences explaining everything}
✅ Good: 1-3 sentences with one specific example or concrete detail

## Writing Standards

**Language:**
- Use "you" (second person)
- No business jargon (leverage, optimize, synergies)
- No weak words (might, could, maybe, probably)
- Short sentences
- Active voice

**Tangibility:**
- Numbers over vague language
- Specific scenarios over general statements
- Concrete imagery over abstractions

**Length:**
- Under 1000 words per email
- Each section roughly equal length
- Scannable (use bullets, headers, line breaks)

## Complete 1/3/1 Example

**Opener:** "Did you know most college students raise way too much money in their first round?"

**Explanation (3 sentences):**
"They see tech headlines about $10M Series As and think that's normal. They pitch VCs with a slide deck asking for $2M before they've made a single sale. The VCs smile, nod, and never respond."

**Conclusion:** "This mistake costs you 6 months of wasted pitches and zero traction."

---

Result: 1 opener + 3 explanation + 1 conclusion = Complete 1/3/1 block
