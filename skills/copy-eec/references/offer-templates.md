# Offer Creation Templates

## The 6-Step Framework

**Goal:** Create offers so specific and tangible that people must opt in.

### Step 1: Base Template
Start with: **"Biggest Mistakes People Make {Doing X}"**

Example: "Biggest Mistakes People Make Fundraising For Startups"

### Step 2: Specify WHO
Replace "people" with your specific audience.

Example: "Biggest Mistakes College Entrepreneurs Make Fundraising For Startups"

### Step 3: List 5 Mistakes
Brainstorm the 5 biggest mistakes your audience makes. Be specific.

Example:
1. Trying to raise too much money in the first round
2. Creating a pitch deck before validating the idea
3. Going straight to VCs instead of starting with angel investors
4. Raising money without a clear plan to profitability
5. Pitching in an industry they don't understand

### Step 4: Map To Outcomes
For each mistake, identify the painful negative outcome it causes.

Format: `{"mistake": "...", "outcome": "..."}`

Example:
- Mistake 1 → "Scaring away investors by looking too ambitious"
- Mistake 2 → "Wasting 3 months on slides nobody wants to see"
- Mistake 3 → "Getting rejected 50+ times before realizing VCs won't touch pre-revenue"
- Mistake 4 → "Running out of cash in 6 months with no path forward"
- Mistake 5 → "Investors seeing through your lack of domain knowledge instantly"

### Step 4B: Select Top 3
Pick the 3 most painful, tangible outcomes.

**Tangibility criteria:**
- Numbers (time, money, quantity)
- Specific imagery (concrete scenarios)
- Immediate recognition ("I've felt this")

Example: Pick outcomes #1, #3, #5

### Step 4C: Bake Into Offer
Format: "Biggest Mistakes {Audience} Make {Topic} That Lead To {Outcome 1}, {Outcome 2}, And {Outcome 3}"

Example: "Biggest Mistakes College Entrepreneurs Make Fundraising That Lead To Scaring Away Investors, Getting Rejected 50+ Times, And Investors Seeing Through Your Lack Of Knowledge"

### Step 5: Name It
Use a "golden word" to create the course name.

**Golden words:** Roadmap, Blueprint, System, Guide, Playbook, Toolkit, Checklist, Accelerator, Plan, Map

Format: "The {Audience}'s {Golden Word}"

Example: "The College Entrepreneur's Roadmap"

### Step 6: Add The Whisper
Append: **(And How To Solve Them)**

Final: "The College Entrepreneur's Roadmap: Biggest Mistakes College Entrepreneurs Make Fundraising That Lead To Scaring Away Investors, Getting Rejected 50+ Times, And Investors Seeing Through Your Lack Of Knowledge (And How To Solve Them)"

## Bonus Refinements

### Remove Duplicates
Check for repeated words between name and subtitle. Reframe if found.

❌ "The Founder's Guide: Biggest Mistakes Founders Make..."
✅ "The Founder's Roadmap: Biggest Mistakes Entrepreneurs Make..."

### Make Outcomes Tangible
Replace vague language with specifics.

❌ "Wasting time on pitches" 
✅ "3 months on slides nobody wants to see"

❌ "Crickets from investors"
✅ "Investors leaving you on 'read' via text"

❌ "Nobody takes you seriously"
✅ "$0 raised after 6 months"

### Add Final Whisper (Optional)
Whisper the final objection: **(Even If {Objection})**

Example: "...And How To Solve Them (Even If You Have No Network)"

## The Soul-Stare Test

Read your offer out loud. Would someone in your target audience:
1. Feel like you're reading their mind?
2. Say "how did you know?"
3. Opt in immediately?

If not, your outcomes aren't tangible enough.

## Quick Examples

**B2B SaaS → Twitter Growth:**
- Audience: B2B SaaS founders
- Topic: Growing on Twitter
- Name: "The B2B SaaS Founder's Twitter Roadmap"
- Subtitle: "Biggest Mistakes B2B SaaS Founders Make On Twitter That Lead To Posting Daily With 0 Engagement, Spending 2 Hours/Day For 3 Months With Nothing To Show, And Looking Like Every Other 'Thought Leader' (And How To Solve Them)"

**Solo Founders → Product Launch:**
- Audience: Solo founders
- Topic: Launching products
- Name: "The Solo Founder's Launch Blueprint"
- Subtitle: "Biggest Mistakes Solo Founders Make Launching Products That Lead To Building For 6 Months Before Realizing Nobody Wants It, $0 Revenue After Launch Day, And Spending More Time 'Building' Than Getting Customers (And How To Solve Them)"
