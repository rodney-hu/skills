# Angle: Offer Validation / Market Research

## When to Use
When testing a new offer, segment, or positioning before a full launch. The goal is feedback and signal, not to close. This is the Feedback/Beta playbook from the SOP.

This angle works because it lowers resistance (no commitment required), surfaces real objections, and often converts into genuine conversations when the problem resonates.

---

## Core Framing
**Problem Frame → What You're Testing → Ask for Perspective (Not Business)**

You're not pitching. You're researching. The email should feel like a peer asking a smart question, not a vendor looking for a yes.

If it reads like a pitch in disguise, rewrite it. The goal is to find out if the problem is real, not to convince them you've solved it.

---

## Structure (One Sentence Per Paragraph)

**P1, Problem Frame**
Name the specific problem you're investigating. Make it concrete and specific to their world. One sentence.

**P2, Your Context**
One sentence on what you're testing or building. Brief, no hype. Position it as early-stage or an experiment.

**P3, The Ask**
Ask for their perspective, not their business. Examples:
- "Is this a real challenge for coaches in your space?"
- "Would this kind of approach be useful at your stage?"
- "Happy to share what we're testing if you're curious."

No solution pitch. No proof. Just an invitation to share their experience.

---

## Subject Line for This Angle
Keep it conversational and question-adjacent.
- "quick question for coaches"
- "something I'm testing"
- "a question about [their niche]"

---

## Full Example

> Subject: quick question for coaches
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I'm exploring whether coaches in the personal development space are running into a specific gap, most of their traffic never converts because visitors aren't warm enough when they arrive.
>
> I'm building a system to address this and want to make sure I'm solving something real before I go further with it.
>
> Is that a challenge you've actually run into?
>
> Thanks,
> Rodney

---

## Variation: ICP Discovery (Talking to Potential Clients Before Building)

Use when you want to understand if a market segment has the problem before even building the solution.

> Subject: a question about your audience
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> I've been talking to coaches in the [niche] space about a specific problem, people discover them online but leave before they're warm enough to reach out.
>
> Curious whether that's something you've noticed or actively working on.
>
> Thanks,
> Rodney

---

## Rules for This Angle
- No pitch, no solution mention in the first email
- No proof or social validation (counterproductive here, this isn't a sales email)
- The CTA should invite perspective, not commitment
- Works best with a small, targeted list, not mass outbound
- If they reply, that's when you introduce the offer, not before
