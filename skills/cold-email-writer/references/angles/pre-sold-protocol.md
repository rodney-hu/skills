# Angle: Pre-Sold Protocol Outreach

## Principles (Apply to All Variations)

These are not templates to copy verbatim. They are structural guides. Every email must be written fresh from the research — Trigger, Audience Intelligence, Company Intelligence — and personalized to that specific person.

The tone across all variations is the same: informal, conversational, a concerned peer who noticed something. Not a vendor. Not a pitch. Permissionless or value-first — give before asking.

**Always:**
- Capital I
- One sentence per paragraph, line break after every sentence
- 25–50 words body, 100 word hard cap
- No em dashes, no bold, no bullets, no links in first touch
- No pleasantries, no flattery (unless the compliment is genuinely specific and real)
- Subject: 1–5 words, lowercase, reads like an internal message
- Sign: Thanks, Rodney (first name only)

**Never:**
- Copy these examples word-for-word
- Say "Pre-Sold Protocol" or "Pre-Sold Content"
- Name the deliverable format externally (no "email course", "funnel", "sequence")
- Fill in [brackets] and send — every placeholder must be replaced with real research

## Variation 1: Core Formula (Friction + Asset)

Best for: prospects with a clear conversion gap, where you have or are building an asset for them.

```
noticed [specific conversion gap], which typically means [negative consequence].

I help [their audience] [desired outcome] by [mechanism].

I created a short video breakdown of how it works.

Want me to send it over?
```

**Example (Booking-only trigger, emerging adults audience):**
> Subject: discovery call drop-off
>
> Hi Camika,
>
> noticed your only CTA is a discovery call, which typically means most visitors leave before they're warm enough to book.
>
> I help young adults navigating major life transitions find direction and clarity before they know they need a coach.
>
> I created a short video breakdown of how it works.
>
> Want me to send it over?
>
> Thanks,
> Rodney

## Variation 2: Icebreaker + Compliment + Offer

Best for: prospects where something specific in their content, branding, or approach is genuinely noteworthy. The compliment must be real and specific — not flattery. If you can't find something genuine, use a different variation.

```
[One sentence: a specific, real observation about something they're doing well — their framing, their niche, their content style. Not a generic compliment.]

noticed [relevant conversion gap], which typically means [negative consequence].

I built something that helps [their audience] [desired outcome] — want me to send it over?
```

**Example (YouTube coach, strong niche positioning):**
> Subject: your framework for high achievers
>
> Hi Marcus,
>
> The way you break down identity-level change for high performers is one of the clearer frameworks I've seen in this space.
>
> noticed your YouTube channel has no mechanism to move viewers into a list you own, which typically means the algorithm controls your reach more than you do.
>
> I built something that helps your audience stay connected to your work beyond the feed — want me to send it over?
>
> Thanks,
> Rodney

## Variation 3: Scarcity / Specific Selection

Best for: when you want to position the outreach as selective rather than broadcast. Creates mild urgency without pressure. Works when you're genuinely working with a small batch.

```
I'm working with [X] [type of business] this [time period] to [solve specific problem] — [their company] fits exactly what I'm looking for.

noticed [specific observation] which is one of the signals I look for.

Want me to send over how it works?
```

**Example (coaching niche, YouTube trigger):**
> Subject: working with 5 coaches this quarter
>
> Hi Sarah,
>
> I'm selectively working with five coaches in the personal development space this quarter to help their audiences move from passive viewers to active subscribers.
>
> noticed your YouTube channel has no email capture layer, which is exactly the gap I look for.
>
> Want me to send over how it works?
>
> Thanks,
> Rodney

## Variation 4: Market Research / Interview Request

Best for: early ICP validation, testing a new segment, or when a warm conversation is more valuable than a yes. No pitch — genuine research framing. The goal is a conversation, not a conversion.

```
noticed [specific observation about their situation or audience].

I'm talking to [X] [type of business/coach] in [their niche] about how they're handling [specific challenge] — [reason this person is relevant].

Would you be open to sharing how you're thinking about it?
```

**Example:**
> Subject: question about your audience
>
> Hi James,
>
> noticed you work specifically with professionals navigating a career transition into purpose-driven work, which is a niche I'm actively researching.
>
> I'm talking to a handful of coaches in this space about how they're keeping that audience engaged between sessions and during the decision-making phase.
>
> Would you be open to sharing how you're thinking about it?
>
> Thanks,
> Rodney

## Variation 5: Pre-Built Asset (Asset Created Upfront)

Best for: when you've already built something specific for this prospect — or when leading with the deliverable is the hook. The asset does the qualifying.

```
noticed [observation], which typically means [consequence].

I built [specific asset description] to help [them or their audience] [avoid problem / achieve desire].

Want me to send it over?
```

Or with a video breakdown:

```
noticed [observation], which typically means [consequence].

I put together a 2-minute breakdown showing exactly how to fix it.

Want me to send you the video?
```

**Example (pre-built, no-opt-in trigger):**
> Subject: built something for your site
>
> Hi Lauren,
>
> noticed your site sends visitors straight to a high-ticket offer with no capture layer, which typically means you're only converting the people who are already ready to buy.
>
> I built a content sequence designed for coaches in your space that captures and warms the rest.
>
> Want me to send it over?
>
> Thanks,
> Rodney

## Variation 6: Audience-First, Asset-First

Best for: when the prospect's audience is the entry point, not their conversion gap. Lead with who they serve and what those people struggle with. The asset is framed as something their audience benefits from.

```
noticed [observation about their target audience or who they help].

One of the challenges [their audience] face is [specific challenge they navigate].

I built [asset] to help them [achieve desired outcome] — you can share it with your [clients/audience].

Want me to send it over?
```

**Example (emerging adults niche):**
> Subject: for the people you work with
>
> Hi Camika,
>
> noticed you work specifically with young adults navigating one of the hardest stretches of early life, the stretch between who they were and who they're becoming.
>
> One of the biggest challenges they face is not having a structured way to move through that transition before they're ready to commit to coaching.
>
> I built a content sequence to help them find their footing on their own terms — you can share it with your audience.
>
> Want me to send it over?
>
> Thanks,
> Rodney

## Choosing the Right Variation

| Signal | Best Variation |
|---|---|
| Clear conversion gap, asset ready or buildable | Variation 1 (Core) |
| Prospect has something genuinely impressive you can point to | Variation 2 (Icebreaker) |
| Small batch outreach, selective positioning | Variation 3 (Scarcity) |
| Testing a new segment, no hard pitch yet | Variation 4 (Research) |
| Asset already built, lead with the deliverable | Variation 5 (Pre-Built) |
| Audience is the strongest entry point | Variation 6 (Audience-First) |

If multiple variations could work, default to Variation 1 or 6. The core formula is the most consistent and easiest to personalize well.
