# Offer Build

Transforms a raw service idea into a complete, deployable, premium-positioned offer stack.
Built from the Packaging & Pricing Masterclass framework — the same system used to build
ghostwriting agencies generating $2,000–$3,000/article and multi-six-figure annual revenue.

## Core Philosophy

> Whoever sets the frame wins the pricing game.

Clients don't buy services. They buy problems solved and outcomes generated.
The service is the vehicle. The offer is what makes them pay a premium for it.

Two providers doing identical work — one charges $80/hr, one charges $7,000/month.
The difference is never the work. It is always the frame.

## Required Inputs

Before building, confirm you have:

```
1. SERVICE: What does the provider actually do?
2. AUDIENCE: Who specifically is the target client? (industry, role, archetype)
3. PROBLEM: What specific problem does this solve?
4. OUTCOME: What does the client ultimately want?
5. PROVIDER_CAPACITY: How many clients can they serve? (for price escalation)
```

If any of these are missing, ask before proceeding. Do not guess the audience or problem —
these are the foundation everything else is built on.

## The Build Sequence

Execute in order. Each step feeds the next.

### STEP 1 — Niche Positioning

Produce a one-sentence positioning statement:
> "I do [SPECIFIC WAY] to solve [SPECIFIC PROBLEM] for [SPECIFIC PERSON]."

**Validate against three levers:**
- **Different problem**: Is the problem specific, not generic? ("drive email subscribers" not "grow audience")
- **Different person**: Is the audience named, not broad? ("fintech founders" not "business owners")
- **Different way**: Is the mechanism specific? ("LinkedIn carousels" not "social content")

If any lever is generic → push for specificity before continuing.

**Information advantage check:** Does the provider have a genuine head start in this niche?
If not, identify the fastest path to credibility: prior clients, industry background, accelerated study.

---

### STEP 2 — V5 Irresistible Offer Script

Build the offer script in five iterations. Do not skip levels — each layer compounds trust.

```
V1: I [SERVICE] for [AUDIENCE] to help them [BASIC OUTCOME].

V2: Most [AUDIENCE] struggle with [SPECIFIC PROBLEM].
    I [SERVICE] to solve that.

V3: Most [AUDIENCE] struggle with [PROBLEM].
    The reason is [ROOT CAUSE].
    I [SERVICE] to solve that root cause.

V4: Most [AUDIENCE] struggle with [PROBLEM].
    The reason is [ROOT CAUSE].
    When this isn't solved, [CONSEQUENCE CHAIN] until [ULTIMATE NEGATIVE OUTCOME].
    I [SERVICE] to prevent that.

V5 (FULL SCRIPT — deployment target):
    Most [AUDIENCE] struggle with [PROBLEM].
    The reason is [ROOT CAUSE].
    When this happens, [CONSEQUENCE 1], which creates [CONSEQUENCE 2],
    which means [CONSEQUENCE 3], until [ULTIMATE NEGATIVE OUTCOME].

    What I do is [SERVICE].
    The reason I specialize in this is because it is the best way to [SOLVE ROOT CAUSE].
    The benefit is [DIRECT BENEFIT].
    Which means [BENEFIT OF BENEFIT].
    All of which leads to [ULTIMATE POSITIVE OUTCOME].
```

**Why each level matters:**
- V1–V2: Most providers stop here. Instant commodity.
- V3: Stating the root cause builds trust automatically — the client believes you have the solution.
- V4: The consequence chain creates urgency. The cost of inaction becomes real.
- V5: The full arc. Problem → root cause → consequence → service → benefit chain → promised land.

### STEP 3 — Productized Package Construction

**Core rule:** One price. One named package. No line items. No hourly rates.

**The SaaS mental model:** Pretend you are a SaaS pricing page. Two tiers. Clear features per tier.
The client clicks a plan. They do not hire a resource.

#### Build the Master Deliverables List

Ask: *"If I were to truly put this client's [FUNCTION] on complete autopilot, what would I do?"*

Organize into four categories:
1. **Core deliverables** — the primary work
2. **Supporting deliverables** — what makes the core work better
3. **Strategic deliverables** — analytics, optimization, iteration
4. **Infrastructure deliverables** — setup, templates, systems

List everything. Do not self-censor. The length of this list IS the pricing argument.

#### Name the Package

Formula: Name should reference the problem solved, the audience, or the outcome generated.

```
Generic:   "Content Package"         → No pricing power
Specific:  "Authority Builder"        → Moderate
Outcome:   "Deal Flow Engine"         → High
Problem:   "Newsletter Autopilot"     → High
```

Keep it 2–4 words. The name signals: "This is a system I repeat. Not custom work."

#### Split into Two Tiers

```
TIER 1 (Basic):
  Items: Top half of master list (highest-impact, most essential)
  Audience: Clients earlier in their journey, smaller budgets
  Price: $[X]/month

TIER 2 (Ferrari):
  Items: Everything in Tier 1 + advanced/strategic/infrastructure deliverables
  Audience: Clients who want maximum leverage and speed
  Price: $[2–3X]/month
```

**Ferrari rule:** Even if no one buys Tier 2, it must exist.
Its job is to make Tier 1 feel cheap.
High-budget clients sort by "most expensive = best" — they need something to buy.

### STEP 4 — Intentional Pricing

**Never:** Random number. Never: Copy what competitors charge.
**Always:** Answer two questions first.

**Q1: What is this worth to ME?**
Run the income math:
```
Target monthly income ÷ price per client = clients needed
```
If 3 clients at the chosen price hits the target → that is a viable floor.

**Q2: What is this worth to the CLIENT?**
Calculate or estimate:
- How much time does this save them? (× their hourly rate)
- How much money could this generate for them?
- What would it cost to hire internally?

Correct price = fraction of what it's worth to the client, above what it's worth to you.

#### Price Anchoring

Before stating the price, tie it to the client's most valued outcome:

| Client Type | Anchor Question | Anchor Point |
|---|---|---|
| Venture-backed founder | "How much are you trying to raise?" | Next funding round |
| B2B SaaS / service business | "What's your average client LTV?" | Pipeline value |
| Newsletter creator | "What do sponsors pay per 1K readers?" | Ad revenue per milestone |
| Investor | "What does one deal mean to your portfolio?" | Deal flow value |
| Coach / consultant | "How many new clients per month = ROI positive?" | Client acquisition value |

Script: *"You're trying to [ACHIEVE OUTCOME]. Over six months, you'll invest [PRICE]. If our work even partially increases the likelihood of [THAT OUTCOME] — do you see how that's ROI positive?"*

#### Pricing Window

```
FLOOR:    $2,000/month or per project
CEILING:  $10,000/month (above this, sales complexity increases significantly)
```

Below $2,000: Almost always a money belief problem, not a market problem.
Above $10,000: Requires contracts, longer sales cycles, deeper delivery infrastructure.

#### Tier Pricing Logic

**Same service / more volume (e.g., 2 newsletters vs. 4):**
→ Give a per-unit price BREAK on the higher tier. Forces a "better deal" decision.
```
Basic:   2 units × $1,500/unit = $3,000/month
Premium: 4 units × $1,250/unit = $5,000/month  ← lower per unit
```

**Different services bundled (e.g., newsletter + social):**
→ Price at a TRUE PREMIUM. No per-unit break. Different work cannot be multiplied.
```
Tier 1:  Newsletter Autopilot    → $3,500/month
Tier 2:  Newsletter + Social     → $7,000/month  ← not discounted
```

#### Volume Bundle Discount

When client wants multiple services → apply 10–20% bundle discount:
```
Service A + B standalone: $8,000/month
Bundle price:             $6,500/month
Script: "Since you're giving me volume across everything, I'm happy to meet you in the middle."
```

### STEP 5 — Six-Month Project Framing

Never present an ongoing service as "month-to-month forever."
Frame it as a project with a defined arc, a midpoint, and a promised end state.

**Standard framing:**
> "To [ACHIEVE GOAL], build a [ASSET TYPE] that will [COMPOUND VALUE LONG-TERM],
> this will be a six-month building process.
> Six months from now you will have [SPECIFIC DELIVERABLE VOLUME + ASSET DESCRIPTION].
> This foundation will pay dividends in [REACH / REVENUE / NETWORK] for years."

Adjust timeline to 3 months for sprint-style offers, 6 months as the default.

---

### STEP 6 — Prepayment Offer

Introduce only AFTER the client has confirmed buy-in on the engagement.
Present as an option — not a condition.

```
Script:
"Since we're aligned on the six-month process — I want to give you an option.
If you'd like to prepay the full engagement up front, I'm happy to give you a month free.
[Monthly rate × 6 = total] → prepaid total = [5 months' equivalent].
Either works for me. Just wanted you to have the option."
```

What prepayment achieves:
- Eliminates churn risk for the full term
- Captures full project cash upfront
- Client psychological investment deepens
- Signals business sophistication to the client even if they decline

---

### STEP 7 — Price Escalation Roadmap (if requested)

The 80% Capacity Rule:
```
1. Fill roster to 80% capacity at base rate
2. Hold 20% open
3. Next client inquiry → pitch at next price tier (not base rate)
4. If YES → earnings increase. If NO → no consequence, base roster intact.
5. As base-rate clients churn → replace with higher-tier clients
6. Repeat until full roster is at new tier
7. Raise again
```

The offer stack never changes. Only the confidence to charge more changes.

## Common Failure Modes — Diagnose and Fix

| Symptom | Root Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Client says "too expensive" | Not anchored to outcome value | Re-anchor to full-time hire cost or outcome value |
| Low close rate | Problem framing too generic | Go deeper on V4 → consequence chain |
| Can't justify premium | Offer is task-based, not outcome-based | Rebuild as productized package |
| Clients negotiate price | Ferrari tier doesn't exist | Add Tier 2 immediately |
| Pricing stays flat over time | No escalation strategy | Apply 80% capacity rule |
| Client asks "can't I do this myself?" | Trade frame not established | "What do you value an hour of your time at?" |

---

## Key Rules (Always Apply)

1. One price. One decision. Yes or no. Never line items.
2. State the package name before the deliverables. State the deliverables before the price.
3. Price anchor before price drop. Always.
4. Ferrari does not negotiate. Let the price sit.
5. "Too expensive" means "I don't understand the value yet." Go back to the anchor.
6. Below $2K is almost never a market problem — it's a money belief problem.
7. The offer stack is the architecture. The niche is what you pour into it.
