# Copy Principles

Rules for writing offer landing page copy. Applies to all framing modes. For mode-specific vocabulary (button text, section labels, success state, trust signals), always defer to `references/framing-modes.md` — this file covers the structural copy rules that don't change by mode.

---

## The Core Shift: Experience vs. Passive Consumption

Regardless of framing mode, copy should make the visitor feel like they're signing up for something they'll *do*, not something they'll *receive*. The degree of action implied varies by mode — but all modes lead with participation, not delivery.

| Passive (Avoid) | Active (Use) |
|---|---|
| "You'll receive..." | "Each morning you get..." |
| "This covers..." | "Here's what you'll build / fix / do..." |
| "Topics include..." | "Here's what's waiting for you" |
| "Get access to..." | "Start [doing the thing]" |
| "You'll learn how to..." | "You'll [verb] [specific thing] so that [outcome]" |

The specific active language varies per framing mode. "Do the thing. See the shift." is challenge-specific. "Come ready to move" is sprint-specific. Pull the right opener from `framing-modes.md`.

## Headline Rules

**Each mode has its own headline pattern** in `references/framing-modes.md`. All patterns share these requirements:

**What makes a headline work:**
- Names a specific transformation (not a tool, framework, or mechanism)
- Implies they've been stuck — this time is different
- Creates a time-bounded container (5 days = achievable)

**What kills a headline:**
- Vague transformations: "Grow your business", "Level up", "Get clarity"
- Mechanism-first titles: "The [Framework Name] Bootcamp"
- Passive constructions: "Get better at X", "Learn how to Y"
- Niche jargon the ICA wouldn't use about themselves

## Subheadline Rules

Every mode's subheadline follows the same underlying arc:

```
[Mode-specific opener from framing-modes.md]
By [Day 5 / the end], you'll have [outcome 1], [outcome 2], and [outcome 3]
— without [common frustration].
```

**Three-outcome arc:**
1. **Clarity outcome** — they understand something they didn't before
2. **Asset/system outcome** — they've built, written, or shipped something real
3. **Confidence/momentum outcome** — they feel ready to act

**Tangibility test — every outcome must pass:**
- ❌ "save time" → ✅ "cut your weekly review time in half"
- ❌ "get more leads" → ✅ "book 3 qualified calls without cold outreach"
- ❌ "feel more confident" → ✅ "walk into your next sales call knowing exactly what to say"

**Frustration framing:**
The frustration at the end should be the thing they've been doing wrong that made them land on this page — not just a pain, but a pattern they recognize as their own.

## Offer Naming Rules

The framing word (Challenge / Course / Sprint / etc.) belongs in the topbar, eyebrow, and section labels. The offer title itself should name the transformation, not the format.

**Formula:** `[Transformation Verb phrase] + [Optional: timeframe or context]`

| Mode | Weak | Strong |
|---|---|---|
| challenge | "The GTM Challenge" | "The Pipeline Fix Challenge" |
| course | "The Email Marketing Course" | "The 5-Day Sequence-That-Converts Course" |
| sprint | "The Content Sprint" | "Publish Your First 30 Posts in 5 Days" |
| bootcamp | "The Sales Bootcamp" | "Close Clients Without Feeling Pushy — A 5-Day Bootcamp" |
| workshop | "The Copy Workshop" | "Write a Homepage That Actually Converts" |

**Avoid generic transformation words:** Growth, Success, Level Up, Transformation, Mastery, Clarity (unless it's genuinely the specific outcome — even then, be concrete)

## Day Title Rules

**Pattern:** `[Action verb] + [Specific object] + [Optional constraint/context]`

Day titles should tell the participant what they're *doing* today — not what concept they'll encounter.

**Verb bank by mode:**

| Mode | Verb flavors |
|---|---|
| `challenge` | Fix, Map, Build, Write, Audit, Identify, Send |
| `course` | Understand, Break down, Map, Analyze, Apply |
| `sprint` | Ship, Build, Draft, Launch, Activate, Send |
| `bootcamp` | Drill, Execute, Run, Build, Do, Cut |
| `workshop` | Create, Design, Write, Build, Draft, Produce |

**Day 5 must feel like completion:**
Use a completion verb — Ship, Send, Launch, Publish, Activate. It should feel like the door opening, not the lesson ending.

- ❌ "Keeping the Momentum Going"
- ✅ "Send Your First Outreach Message with Confidence"
- ✅ "Launch Your Automated Welcome Sequence"

## Day Teaser Rules

One sentence. Hints at the result without giving away the method.

**Pattern:** `You'll [verb phrase] so that [mini-outcome].`

**Variants:**
- `By the end of this one, you'll finally [specific thing].`
- `This is the one most people skip — and it's why [problem persists].`
- `One [timeframe] [task/exercise/drill] that changes [how audience does thing].`

**Tone:** Conversational across all modes. Should feel like a friend giving a heads-up. Adjust intensity for mode — bootcamp teasers can be more direct/blunt, workshop teasers warmer.

## Author Bio Rules

Consistent across all modes:

**Formula:** `[Credential signal] + [Proof of work] + [Who you help]`

**Rules:**
- Lead with what earns the right to teach this — not a job title, not a value statement
- Middle = something built, solved, or survived — not aspiration
- End = mirror the ICA back at them

**What to avoid:**
- "Passionate about helping..."
- "Dedicated to..."
- "On a mission to..."
- Starting with their name or title

**Tone:** Confident, not boastful. Matter-of-fact, not humble. Consistent regardless of framing mode.

## Secondary CTA Rules

If subscriber count is known → use count variant from `framing-modes.md`.
If not → use no-count fallback from `framing-modes.md`.

Never fabricate a subscriber count. If the count is genuinely unknown and the no-count fallback feels weak, use: `"Built for [SPECIFIC_AUDIENCE] who are done [old behavior]."`

---

## Trust Signal Rules

Trust signals are mode-specific — pull the set of three from `references/framing-modes.md`. Do not mix signals across modes.

The third trust signal (exit signal) always reduces pressure. Adjust formality for brand type:
- Casual: "Bail anytime — no hard feelings" (challenge/sprint)
- Neutral: "Drop out anytime — no friction" (sprint/bootcamp)
- Formal: "Unsubscribe anytime with one click" (course)
- Warm: "Drop out anytime — no pressure" (workshop)

---

## Success State Rules

Use the mode's exact `success title` and `success subtitle` from `references/framing-modes.md`. Do not improvise or blend modes.

The success state creates identity confirmation — it mirrors the action the visitor just took. "Challenge accepted" mirrors "Accept the Challenge." "Sprint started" mirrors "Start the Sprint." The logic must close.

---

## Tone Calibration by Brand Type

Base tone comes from the selected framing mode. Adjust for brand voice signals:

| Brand Voice | Adjustment |
|---|---|
| Formal / enterprise | Replace casual exit signal with formal. Remove slang from microcopy. |
| Casual / creator-led | Keep all defaults. Trust the mode's natural language. |
| High-touch / coaching | Warm up day teasers. Workshop-style even in challenge mode. |
| Technical / operator | Keep day titles precise and task-focused. Avoid vague transformation language. |

Rule: never change the structural framing based on tone. The experience-vs-passive-consumption distinction holds for all brand types and all modes.

## Testimonial Guidance

If testimonials are not available, leave placeholders and note in debrief. Never write fake or composite quotes.

If testimonials ARE provided, apply quality hierarchy:
1. **Outcome quote** — specific result ("I booked 4 calls in the first week after Day 3")
2. **Clarity quote** — specific shift ("I finally understood why my outreach wasn't landing")
3. **Praise quote** — general positive ("Most practical thing I've done all year") — use only if 1 and 2 aren't available

Keep quotes to 1–3 sentences. Specificity builds trust more than enthusiasm.
