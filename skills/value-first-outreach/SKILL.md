# Value-First Outreach

Takes one company and produces one outreach brief. The point: show a useful, specific
thing before asking for anything. Relevance earns the reply, not polish.

**Done =** one markdown brief per company, saved as `outreach-brief-[company].md`.
Not "researching," not "planning." If the verdict is PARK, done = a one-line reason.

## Inputs

- **Company:** name or URL
- **Offer:** what Rodney sells or can demonstrate. If it isn't clear from the conversation, ask once, then continue.
- **Channel (optional):** DM, email, or Loom. Default to Loom with a DM cover message.

Check for a niche "winners" note from earlier in the conversation before researching one.

## Phase 1: Find one signal

Search public sources: job postings, recent launches or announcements, founder or team
posts, public help docs, customer reviews, the public customer journey. Record the source
URL and date for each thing you use.

- Prefer an explicit priority or complaint over an inferred gap.
- Funding, growth, and mission fit are not signals. They only put a company on the list.
- Stop at the first signal that is specific, sourced, and checkable. More research does not make a weak signal stronger.

## Phase 2: Gate (build or park)

Build only if all four are true:

1. There is a specific sourced observation
2. It connects to something Rodney can already demonstrate
3. There is one identifiable person who owns the outcome
4. A useful change can be shown in one sitting, without internal access

If any is false, the verdict is **PARK**. Write one line on why and stop. Do not invent a
diagnosis to justify building. A parked company costs nothing; a forced demo costs trust.

Keep three statements separate, because blending them is how outreach starts to sound like an accusation:

- **Observation:** what the source actually says or shows
- **Hypothesis:** what difficulty might follow, stated with its uncertainty
- **Demonstration:** what the asset shows (not what it proves about results)

## Phase 3: Winners pattern

Find 2-3 similar companies already getting the result. Pull one pattern, with sources.
Share it as an idea ("here's what others do"), never as a score or a gap callout. Do it
once per niche and reuse it. Only the line about this company is new each time.

## Phase 4: Scope the asset

Fill this in. The asset is the smallest thing that proves the insight: one page, one
list, one mockup, one sample, or three posts.

- Source and observation
- Hypothesis and uncertainty
- Outcome owner (one person; the founder at small companies, the functional owner at larger ones)
- Input, process, visible output
- Human review or fallback
- Link plan (must open with no login)

Rules for the asset: public or synthetic data only. Label mockups and simulations. No
private systems, no test messages to their customers, no implied affiliation. For
healthcare or mental-health companies, stay with nonclinical operations and education.
Spec the asset here. Build it only if Rodney asks.

## Phase 5: Write the pack

Read `references/templates.md` for templates and a worked example. Produce:

- Recommended channel and owner (public professional email, or LinkedIn if they are active there). One person, never a blast.
- Loom script (4 beats: noticed, winners, built, offer)
- Cover message: the DM as 2-3 separate short messages (see `references/templates.md`), plus a one-message email version
- One follow-up

Copy rules, and why they matter:

- Plain words, short sentences, written like a text to a colleague. Fancy copy reads as a pitch.
- Lowercase formatting for all copy (DMs, emails, Loom scripts, follow-ups, subject lines, sign-offs): every letter is lowercase except the letter I, including I'm, I'll, I can. That means sentence starts, names, and company names are lowercase too ("hey sam", "rodney"). "lmk" is allowed as shorthand for "let me know" and stays lowercase. This is how Rodney writes, and it reads like a text from a person instead of a template. This applies to the copy only. The brief's own headings and notes stay in normal case.
- Lead with the signal and the asset. No mission praise, biography, or tool list.
- Be specific in the copy. Name the actual buyer, audience, or thing the asset is about, not "your buyers" or "your best buyer." Say "a list of 25 mid-size logistics companies hiring dispatchers," not "25 companies that fit your best buyer." A specific line shows the research was real and gives them something to react to. If the buyer is unclear, find it in Phase 1 before writing.
- DMs on LinkedIn and X go out as 2-3 separate short messages, not one block. That is how Rodney actually talks on those platforms, and it reads like a text from a person. Pattern: (1) the asset and link, (2) what it could do for them, with one or two concrete uses, (3) the offer, on its own. Each message is one or two short lines. Stagger the sends instead of firing all at once. Email stays one message.
- In a DM, the signal can be implied when the asset explains itself. Name it in message 2 when it would not be obvious why they are getting this.
- Make the "what it could do" line concrete and specific to their world (for example, "keep students logging in after week two"), phrased as a possibility with "could" or "might," never a diagnosis.
- No claims about saved hours, conversion, or revenue unless measured. Unmeasured numbers undo the trust the asset earned.
- Share what was seen. Never say what is broken.
- One ask only. First touch has no meeting request. Pick one offer line: "happy to walk you through how I built it," "lmk if you want more info," or "lmk if it's relevant." Keep it short and on its own.
- No em dashes, no bold, no hype words in the messages themselves.
- The cover message links to the video on purpose. That is the exception to the usual no-link-in-first-touch rule, because the video is the value.
- Follow-up: once, 4-7 business days later, and it adds something new (a screenshot, a takeaway). Never "bumping this." Then stop unless there is a genuinely new signal.

## Phase 6: Self-check before delivering

- Signal is specific, sourced, and dated
- Observation, hypothesis, demonstration are kept apart
- No unmeasured claims
- Asset link opens without a login
- Loom under 2:30
- DM is 2-3 messages, each one or two short lines, offer last and on its own
- All copy is lowercase except the letter I (check subject lines, names, company names, acronyms, and sign-offs too). The letter I is the only capital. "lmk" stays lowercase.
- Copy names the specific buyer or audience, not "your buyers"
- One owner, one ask
- Would Rodney reply to this if he received it?

Fix anything that fails, then save the brief.

## Brief format

```
# [Company] Outreach Brief
**Verdict:** BUILD | PARK (one line why)
**Owner / channel:**
**Signal:** [source URL, date]
**Observation:** / **Hypothesis:** / **Demonstration:**
**Winners pattern:** [sources]
**Asset spec:** [fields from Phase 4]
**Loom script:**
**Cover message (DM):**
**Cover message (email):**
**Follow-up:**
**Next action:** [the one thing Rodney does next]
```
